# OrgForms — 2–3 minute pitch script

Hello, I’m Deepak, the solo developer of OrgForms and an alumnus of Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya, Alwar.

Small organizations collect information every day: registrations, membership details, requests and bookings. Often, each workflow becomes another form, spreadsheet and set of messages. Staff repeat questions and move answers between tools. When the interaction needs an item card, quantity or preference, a basic text field is not enough.

I’m building OrgForms to let these teams configure their own collection interfaces. It combines organization workspaces, a dynamic form builder and a Library of reusable data and grouped UI elements. A creator can combine display text, images and answer fields into a component, reuse it in forms, publish the form and review structured responses. Optional local AI through Ollama assists with drafting, while the user reviews the result before saving.

For example, a community library can create membership or seat-request forms and review the responses in one workspace. A service business can reuse item cards with quantities and preferences. These examples are development scenarios, not customer deployments.

The working prototype uses Django, schema-based validation and organization access controls. My proposed differentiation is reusable interfaces and data together with optional local inference. I still need to prove that this combination solves a meaningful customer problem.

I am at the prototype stage. I have no external users, pilots, revenue or customer interviews yet. My next two milestones are to stabilize the core workflows and then interview prospective users and run small pilots, initially with accessible organizations in Jaipur.

A possible business model is subscriptions for hosted workspaces and setup or support for local deployments. Pricing remains unvalidated.

Through NAV UDAY, I’m seeking mentorship, pilot introductions and support to turn this working project into a product that real organizations choose to use. Thank you.
